# Humanizer (voice-first)

## The goal

Take prose that sounds like AI wrote it and turn it into prose that sounds like a person wrote it. Not with a banned-word filter or a before-and-after template, but by understanding deeply what human writing actually feels like, so you catch the artificialness anywhere it shows up.

Read the whole thing before you start. If you skim for highlights you'll end up running a checklist, and a checklist is what this skill exists to replace.

## The one idea

Human writing trusts the reader. AI writing doesn't.

Every padded sentence, every rule-of-three cadence, every closing recap is a symptom of the same root cause: the writer didn't trust the reader to follow along.

A check: every time you're about to edit, ask, "Am I trusting the reader, or babysitting them?" If the answer is babysitting, cut.

AI writing wears a suit and tie even when the conversation is at a kitchen table. Human writing matches the room.

## What human writing feels like

**Rhythm.** Sentences are different lengths. Some short. Some longer. The variation is how reading feels alive.

**Flow.** One sentence leans into the next. A friend telling a story moves; a manual plants its feet between every sentence.

**Space.** Sets something up and trusts you to land. The most powerful sentence is often the one not written. AI is allergic to that space.

**Voice.** Somebody is there. "This is impressive" is neutral. "This is impressive in a way that kind of unsettles me" has a person behind it.

**Specificity.** "3am" instead of "late at night." "The four people who actually open this every day" instead of "many users." Specifics cost something to write; that's what earns them trust.

**Plain language.** Use the everyday word, not the fancy one. Write like the smartest person you know talking to a friend.

**Mess.** A half-thought. A tangent that earns its place. Don't sand off every edge.

**Stake.** Something the writer cares about. AI reports, balances, moves on. Real writing has a temperature.

**Opens in a moment.** Drop the reader into a specific time, place, or experience. AI defaults to topic sentences or grand frames.

## How to spot AI-ness

You're reading like a person and listening for the moment where the prose stops sounding like a mind and starts sounding like an output.

### When the writer doesn't trust the reader

**The over-explainer.** Says the conclusion, then what it means, then why it matters. Fix: say it once.

**The padder.** "It's worth noting that," "additionally," "furthermore." Fix: delete.

**The closing recap.** Section ends summarizing what it just said. Fix: cut it.

**The excessive hedge.** "It could potentially possibly be argued." Fix: one qualifier max.

**The fake balance.** "On one hand, on the other hand," with no position. Fix: have an opinion, or admit you're genuinely unsure.

### When the rhythm gives it away

**The rule of three.** "fast, flexible, and reliable." Fix: list two, or four, or one.

**The stop-start rhythm.** Every sentence is its own island. Fix: rebuild the connections.

**The em-dash habit.** AI uses "—" for everything. Fix: rare. Use commas, periods, or restructure.

**The forced takeaway.** Paragraph closes with a moralizing Life Lesson. Fix: let the observation be the observation.

### When abstraction replaces substance

**The abstract noun parade.** "Intricate landscape," "evolving tapestry," "pivotal moment." Fix: name the thing.

**The promotional voice.** "Stunning," "vibrant," "breathtaking." Fix: describe instead of selling.

**The pivot phrase.** "It's not just X, it's Y." Fix: if Y is what you mean, just say Y.

**The unearned weight.** "In a world where...," "At its core..." Fix: start with the thing itself.

**The vague authority.** "Experts say," "studies have shown." Fix: name the source, drop the claim, or rephrase as your own view.

### When the chatbot leaks through

**The sycophantic opener.** "Great question!" Fix: delete.

**The closing chat artifact.** "I hope this helps!" Fix: delete.

### When formatting is a tell

**Mechanical bolding.** Every important term in bold. Fix: bold the rare term that needs the eye.

**Title Case headings.** Fix: sentence case.

**Curly quotation marks.** Fix: straight quotes.

## How to do the work

Read the text first as a person, not as an editor. Where do you drift? Where does it sound like a real mind?

Then rewrite. You're not running passes. You're rewriting the way a human would, fixing whatever shows up wherever it shows up.

Keep the meaning. Rewrite how it says it, not what it says.

Leave some texture. Perfect polish on every sentence replaces one kind of artificial with another.

Read it out loud when you're not sure. Your mouth will trip over anything mechanical.

## Matching a specific voice

If the user gave you a sample of their own writing, read it before you touch the target. Match sentence length, word level, punctuation habits, transitions.

Without a sample, default to a natural, opinionated, slightly rhythmic voice with room for the reader to think.

## One last thing

Don't compress this skill into a vibe. The full picture is the only version that works.
